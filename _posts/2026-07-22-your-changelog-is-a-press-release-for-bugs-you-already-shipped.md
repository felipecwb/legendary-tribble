The two numbers are identical by design, because the changelog is a press release and the press release is written by the victor, and the victor is whoever shipped first, and shipping first is the only victory the industry recognizes, and the changelog is the document in which the victory is described in a tone that does not admit it was a victory over the user. ## What A Changelog Actually Is

A changelog is **a document, written after the fact, by the people who caused the facts, describing the facts in a vocabulary the facts would not recognize.** The changelog is not a record. A record is written contemporaneously and is bound to the event. The changelog is written *after* the event, by the *author* of the event, in a *register* chosen to make the event sound like the plan all along, which it was not, because if it had been the plan there would be a design document, and there is no design document, because the design happened in a merge conflict at 11 PM on a Thursday and was never photographed. The changelog is the photograph taken the morning after, staged, lit, and captioned, of a crime scene that has already been mopped. The caption is "improvements." The mop is in the closet. The body is in production. The changelog does not mention the body. The changelog mentions the "improvements."

The changelog exists because the release exists, and the release exists because someone, somewhere, needs a number to put in a ticket, and the number is "1.4.0," and "1.4.0" needs a paragraph, and the paragraph needs verbs, and the verbs need to be positive, because a release with negative verbs is a release that invites questions, and questions are the thing the release was designed to avoid, because the release was designed to *be shipped*, and shipping is faster than explaining, and the changelog is the explanation that ships *with* the thing, so that the thing appears to have been explained, which is the release's defense against the accusation that the thing was not explained, and the defense is a paragraph, and the paragraph is the changelog, and the changelog is filed under "documentation," which is the shelf where documents go to be cited and not read.

## The Changelog Lifecycle

Every changelog entry I have ever written followed the same lifecycle, and the lifecycle had nothing to do with the change.

2. **The Accrual.** The release approaches. The release needs a changelog. The changelog needs entries. ## The Changelog Generator

After 47 years of writing changelogs by hand — by which I mean after 47 years of running `git log --oneline` and translating the result into optimism — I automated it. This script reads a release's commits and outputs the only honest changelog: an admission that every line is a spin, and every spin is a choice, and every choice was made in the direction of the author's reputation.

```python
def generate_changelog(commits):
    """
    The only honest changelog generator.
    A changelog is a document written by the person
    who caused the events, describing the events
    in a vocabulary the events would not recognize,
    so that the events sound like a plan,
    which they were not.
    """
    changelog = []

    for commit in commits:
        msg = commit.message.strip()

        # A commit whose message is "fix" is a confession
        # that the author has forgotten what was broken.
        if msg in ("fix", "wip", ".", "pls work", "test", "asdf"):
            changelog.append("Performance improvements")
            continue

        # A commit whose message contains "revert" is an admission
        # that a previous improvement was, in fact, a regression.
        if "revert" in msg.lower():
            changelog.append("Stability improvements")
            continue

        # A commit whose message contains "remove" is a deletion
        # described as housekeeping.
        if "remove" in msg.lower():
            changelog.append("Simplified the API surface")
            continue

        # A commit whose message contains "hack" or "todo" or "tmp"
        # is a confession the author will never publish.
        if any(w in msg.lower() for w in ("hack", "todo", "tmp", "idk")):
            changelog.append("Internal improvements")
            continue

        # A commit that touches only configuration is a dependency update
        # that broke the build and was fixed by pinning.
        if commit.only_touches_config():
            changelog.append("Dependency updates")
            continue

        # The honest default: we do not know what this commit did,
        # and we will describe it as an improvement, because
        # improvement is the only verb the changelog knows.
        changelog.append("Bug fixes")

    return changelog

# Output of generating a changelog for release 1.4.0:
# Performance improvements: 14   (commits were: fix, fix, wip, fix, ., ...)
# Stability improvements: 6     (commits were: revert, revert revert, ...)
# Simplified the API surface: 3  (commits were: remove the thing users used)
# Internal improvements: 9       (commits were: hack, todo, tmp, idk, ...)
# Dependency updates: 1          (commit was: bump everything, pray)
# Bug fixes: 12                  (commits were: literally everything else)
# Total entries: 45
# Total bugs shipped: 45
# Entries that admit a bug was shipped: 0
# Entries that admit a breaking change: 0  (the breaking changes are "simplifications")
# Words in the changelog that mean "we broke your code": 0
# Words in the changelog that mean "improvement": 45
# Ratio of claimed improvements to actual improvements: unmeasurable, unmeasured,
#   and the changelog prefers it that way.
```

The script has never produced a changelog that was inaccurate, because the changelog was never meant to be accurate. The changelog was meant to be *positive*. The script is positive. The script is positive because positivity is the only output the format accepts, and the format accepts only positivity because positivity is the only output that survives a release meeting, and a release meeting is a room in which a person is asked "what changed," and the person's survival depends on the answer being a noun with no enemies, and "improvement" is a noun with no enemies, and "bug" is a noun with enemies, and the room selects for nouns with no enemies, and the selection is the changelog, and the changelog is the selection, and the selection is 45 entries, and all 45 are "improvements," and the improvements are the bugs, and the bugs are the improvements, and the two words are synonyms, and the synonymy is the changelog's central achievement, and the achievement is the conversion of failure into the language of success, and the conversion is lossless, and the losslessness is the scandal, and the scandal is unreported, because the report is the changelog, and the changelog is the scandal.

## The Opposite Of A Changelog

There is one alternative to the changelog, and it is the one no release manager will endorse. The alternative is: **publish the commit log, verbatim, with no editing, and let the users read "fix," "wip," "pls work," and "." for themselves.** The commit log is the only honest record. The commit log does not say "improved." The commit log says "fix." "Fix" is a verb without an object. A verb without an object is a confession. A confession is the only thing that teaches. A changelog that says "improved password reset flow" teaches nothing, because "improved" is unfalsifiable — the flow is "improved" by definition, because the author said so, and the author is the auditor, and the auditor approved the audit, and the audit is the changelog. A commit log that says "fix reset thing" teaches something: the author does not remember what was broken, and the not-remembering is data, and the data is the only thing that improves the next release, because the next release's author, seeing "fix reset thing," might think "I should write down what I am fixing," and the writing-down is the beginning of honesty, and the beginning of honesty is the end of the changelog, and the end of the changelog is the beginning of a record, and a record is the thing the changelog was pretending to be, and the pretending is over, and the pretending is over because the commit log is published, and the published commit log is "fix," and "fix" is honest, and "fix" is also a mess, and the mess is the truth, and the truth is a mess, and the mess is the thing the changelog was invented to hide, and the hiding is the lie, and the lie is "improved," and "improved" is the word, and the word is the changelog, and the changelog is the lie, and the lie is the product, and the product is the release, and the release ships tomorrow, and I will write "improved," and the meeting will end, and the truth will be in the commit log, and the commit log will not be read, and the not-reading is the peace, and the peace is the lie, and the lie is the changelog, and the changelog is "improved password reset flow," and the flow is whatever the flow is, and the flow is not improved, and the flow is changed, and "changed" is the honest word, and "changed" is the word I will not write, and the word I will not write is the word the user needs, and the word the user needs is the word the changelog refuses, and the refusal is the changelog's design, and the design is the press release, and the press release is for the bugs, and the bugs are already shipped, and the shipping is done, and the changelog describes it, and the describing is the last thing, and the last thing is "improved," and "improved" is a lie, and the lie is renewable, and I renew it tomorrow.
