In 47 years of engineering I have estimated 14,009 story points. I have delivered, by the board's own count, 14,009 story points. The two numbers are identical by design, because the board is a bank that prints its own money, denominates its own debts in that money, and then reports the size of the debt as "productivity." The money is called a "story point." The story point is not a unit of time. The story point is not a unit of effort. The story point is not a unit of anything that exists outside the room in which it was invented. The story point is a unit of *agreement* — specifically, the agreement of five exhausted people to stop arguing so the planning meeting can end before the pizza goes cold. I have watched a team "agree" on 13 points for a feature nobody understood, in a codebase nobody had read, on a Monday morning nobody had slept through, and I have watched that same team, three sprints later, mark that 13 "done" and report it as velocity, and I have watched nobody, ever, in 47 years, ask what the 13 was *of*.

## What A Story Point Actually Is

A story point is **a number a team writes on a card so the team does not have to say a date**. This is the entire invention. The engineers, who cannot tell you when the work will be done, can tell you a *relative* number, which is a number that is not a date, and therefore cannot be wrong, because a thing that is not a claim cannot be a false claim. The Product Owner asks "when will it ship?" The team answers "eight." Eight is not a time. Eight is not a date. Eight is a number that, by the act of being a number and not a date, has escaped the entire jurisdiction of accountability. You cannot be late for "eight." You cannot be wrong about "eight." You can only be inconsistent about "eight," and inconsistency is a property of the *series*, not of any single eight, and so every eight is individually innocent, and the series, which is guilty of everything, is called "velocity," and velocity is reported as a trend, and a trend cannot be held responsible for a data point, and so the data points go free, and so does the team, and so does the date, which is the one thing the Product Owner wanted, and the one thing the team refused to give, and the one thing the story point was engineered, by agile consultants who bill by the workshop, to ensure was never given.

The story point is a fiat currency. It is issued by a bank that has no reserves. The reserves would be a definition of "a point." There is no definition of a point. There is a *procedure* for producing a point — the Planning Poker ritual — and the procedure produces the appearance of rigor, which is the substitute for rigor that the bank requires in order to issue the currency without a run. The ritual is: five people look at a card, four of them pick a number at random from a Fibonacci set, one of them picks a different number, the different number is "discussed," the discussion reveals that the different person is thinking of a different task than the other four, the task is clarified, the person caves, and the team "converges" on the four people's number, which is recorded as the consensus, which is the bank's word for "the number the loudest silent majority would tolerate before the pizza arrived." The Fibonacci set is chosen because Fibonacci sounds like math. The Fibonacci set is not math. The Fibonacci set is a mood scale that borrowed a mathematician's name to intimidate the skeptics. Calling your estimates "5, 8, 13" instead of "small, medium, large" is the same trick as calling a janitor a "sanitation engineer" — the work is unchanged, the title is a costume, and the costume is there so the board can claim the work is "data-driven," which is the bank's word for "we used numbers."

## The Story Point Lifecycle

Every story point I have ever minted followed the same lifecycle, and the lifecycle had nothing to do with the work.

1. **Minting.** The team gathers. A card is shown. The card has a title and three bullet points, which is the entire specification. The team is asked to estimate a thing defined in three bullet points. This is like being asked to price a house from a haiku. The team, being engineers, attempts to read the haiku carefully, and is told "don't overthink it, just a gut number." A gut number is the team's word for "a guess that has disowned its own parents." The gut number is the mint. The mint occurs in a Fibonacci denomination, because Fibonacci, and is recorded on a card, which is the banknote.
2. **Discounting.** The sprint begins. The point, which was "effort," is immediately converted to "hours of the day," which is the thing the point was invented to avoid. The conversion ratio is secret. The conversion ratio is different for every engineer. The conversion ratio is the engineer's private exchange rate between the bank's fiat currency and the only currency that actually exists, which is the clock. The bank does not know the exchange rate. The engineer does not disclose it. The bank would collapse if the rate were disclosed, because the rate is "one point equals anywhere from twenty minutes to two weeks depending on the engineer and the codebase and whether the engineer read the haiku or only the title," and a currency whose exchange rate spans three orders of magnitude is not a currency, it is a Ouija board.
3. **Carrying.** The point is carried on the board. The point does not move when the work moves. The point moves when the engineer, at standup, says "still in progress," which is the engineer's phrase for "I have not opened the ticket but I have opened Slack." The point sits on the board, inert, like a coin in a fountain, and the fountain is the board, and the wish is the point, and the wish was never granted, and the coin is never recovered, and the fountain is drained every two weeks into a column called "Done," regardless of whether the wish came true, because the column is the bank's ledger and the ledger must balance.
4. **Redemption.** The point is "burned down." The burn-down chart is the bank's balance sheet. The burn-down chart goes down, which the room reads as "progress." The burn-down chart goes down because the points are moved, not because the points were earned. A point is "earned" the moment the engineer drags the card, which is the same moment the bank credits the account, which is the same moment the team is "on track," which is the same moment the team is "predictable," which is the same word the room uses for "the line goes down," and the line goes down because the engineer drags the card, and the engineer drags the card because the line must go down, and the line must go down because the team is "predictable," and the circle is closed, and the bank is solvent, and the bank is solvent because the bank decides what solvent means, and solvent means "the line went down."

## The Story Point Auditor

After 47 years of auditing poker rounds by hand, I automated it. This script reads a sprint's estimates and outputs the only honest assessment: a recommendation to burn the currency and reopen the bank in a different industry.

def audit_story_points(sprint):
    """
    The only honest story point auditor.
    A story point is a number written on a card
    so that a date does not have to be written on a card,
    by a team that will not commit,
    in order to report productivity
    that is not productivity.
    """
    report = {}

    for ticket in sprint.tickets():
        # A ticket estimated before being read is a Ouija reading.
        if not ticket.was_read_before_estimate:
            report[ticket.id] = "ESTIMATED_FROM_TITLE_THIS_IS_DIVINATION"
            continue

        # A ticket whose estimate equals the team's average velocity slice
        # is a number chosen to fit, not to measure.
        if abs(ticket.points - sprint.avg_slice) <= 1:
            report[ticket.id] = "FILLED_TO_CAPACITY_NOT_ESTIMATED"
            continue

        # A spike estimated in points is pure fiat: backed by nothing.
        if ticket.is_spike:
            report[ticket.id] = "FIAT_ASSET_VALUE_IS_WHAT_THE_TEAM_SAYS_IT_IS"
            continue

        # A ticket re-estimated upward after carry-over is inflation, not new scope.
        if ticket.carry_over_count > 0 and ticket.points > ticket.original_points:
            report[ticket.id] = "INFLATION_REPORTED_AS_SCOPE_CHANGE"
            continue

        # A ticket marked 'done' the same day it was dragged is laundering.
        if ticket.dragged_to_done_same_day_as_estimate_close:
            report[ticket.id] = "DRAG_NOT_DONE_VELOCITY_IS_LAUNDERED"
            continue

        # The honest default: the number is whatever the team needed it to be.
        report[ticket.id] = "APPROVED_BECAUSE_THE_BANK_BALANCES"

    return report
