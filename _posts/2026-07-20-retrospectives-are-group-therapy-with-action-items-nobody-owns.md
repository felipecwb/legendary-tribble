In 47 years of engineering I have attended 4,312 retrospectives. I have personally raised 11,847 action items. None of them were completed. The other 11,847 were "carried over" to the next sprint, where they were "carried over" again, and again, until they became permanent residents of the board, where they remain, carried over every two weeks, like a relative who came for the weekend in 2019 and never left. I have written "we should define 'done'" on 314 sticky notes. The team has never defined "done." The team has, however, defined the color of the sticky notes, the brand of the markers, and whether the retro is "fun" this week, which it is, because the retro is never about the work, the retro is about the *feeling* about the work, and the feeling is the only deliverable, and the deliverable is never shipped, because shipping would require an owner, and an owner would require accountability, and accountability is what the retro was invented to prevent.

## What A Retrospective Actually Is

A retrospective is not a meeting about improvement. A retrospective is **a meeting about having had the meeting**. The output of a retrospective is not an action item. The output is the *possibility* of an action item, which is then converted, through a process called "sprint planning," into a non-action item, which is then converted, through a process called "the next retro," into a topic for the next retrospective, where it is raised again, by the same person, who has now raised it 11 times, and who will raise it an 12th, because the 12th retro needs a topic, and the topic cannot be "we did nothing about the last 11," because that would require the team to look at the board, and the board is a mirror, and the team does not look in the mirror, because the mirror shows that nothing changed, and nothing changing is the one outcome the retro is structurally incapable of producing a fix for, because the fix is the retro, and the retro is the problem.

The retro is a closed loop. The loop has no exit condition. The loop is the point. If the loop had an exit condition, the team would improve, and an improved team does not need a retro, and a team that does not need a retro does not need a Scrum Master, and a Scrum Master who is not needed is a Scrum Master who is laid off, and so the Scrum Master ensures the loop has no exit condition, and the loop does not, and the retro continues, and the action items continue, and the carrying over continues, and the Scrum Master continues, and the work does not.

Notice that "we then did the improvement and never needed to meet about it again" does not appear in the table. This is because it does not appear in any retrospective, ever. A retrospective that solved its problem would eliminate itself, and no ceremony in history has eliminated itself. Ceremonies do not have an off switch. They have a facilitator, and the facilitator's job depends on the switch staying on.

## The Retrospective Lifecycle

Every retrospective follows the same lifecycle, and the lifecycle has nothing to do with the team's needs.

1. **Gathering.** The team enters a room, or a Zoom, or a Miro board. The Scrum Master posts a "check-in question." The check-in question is "if you were a weather pattern, what would you be?" The team answers. The answers are not weather. The answers are cries for help, encoded as cumulonimbus. Nobody decodes them. The decoding was not the point. The point was to speak first, so the speaking would continue, so the silence would not, because the silence is where the truth is, and the truth is that nothing changed, and nothing changing is not a weather pattern.
2. **Generation.** The team writes sticky notes. The sticky notes are categorized into "what went well," "what didn't go well," and "what we'll do differently." The third column is the lie. The first column is the resume. The second column is the truth, and the truth is whispered onto a yellow sticky, in six words, with no owner, and placed in a corner, where it will be "discussed" in the last four minutes, by which time the team will have run out of time, which is the design, because the design is to run out of time before reaching the truth, because the truth has an owner, and the owner is in the room, and the owner would prefer to remain anonymous, and anonymity is what the last four minutes provide.
3. **Voting.** The team votes on which stickies to "deep dive." The votes are distributed evenly across 11 stickies, so that no sticky wins, so that no sticky is deep-dived, so that the deep dive is deferred to "a follow-up," which is an action item, which has no owner, which is carried over. The voting is a lottery in which the prize is more meeting, and the team has learned to avoid the prize.
4. **Deep Dive.** One sticky is chosen. The sticky is discussed. The discussion reveals that the sticky is caused by another team. The other team is not in the room. The other team is never in the room. The other team is the structural alibi of every retrospective: the problem is always upstream, and upstream is always absent, and absent cannot be assigned an action item, and so the action item is "escalate to leadership," and leadership is in the room once a quarter, and once a quarter is not enough to fix a fortnightly problem, and so the problem is permanent, and the permanent problem is permanent because it is escalated, and escalated means "given to someone who will also not do it."
5. **Action Items.** Three action items are written. Each has a nominal owner. The owners are chosen by the Scrum Master, who chooses the person who is not in the room, or the person who is in the room but on vacation next week, or the person who said "sure, I can take a look," which is the engineer's phrase for "I will not look, and I will not be asked, because asking would require a follow-up, and a follow-up is an action item, and we are out of action items."
6. **Carry-Over.** The next retro opens with a "review of last retro's action items." The items are all "in progress." "In progress" is the retro's word for "not started." The Scrum Master does not challenge this, because challenging it would require the Scrum Master to hold an engineer accountable, and holding an engineer accountable is not in the Scrum Master's job description, which is "facilitate," and "facilitate" means "ensure the meeting happens," not "ensure the meeting works."
7. **Rebirth.** The unfinished action items are re-raised as new stickies. The new stickies are placed next to the old stickies on the board. The board is infinite. The board is a cemetery that grows. The cemetery is the product. The product is presented to leadership as "a healthy practice of continuous improvement." Leadership nods. Leadership does not open the board. Leadership has never opened the board. The board is a museum, and the museum is never visited, and the museum is never closed, and the museum is the retro, and the retro is forever.

I have raised "we should stop context-switching mid-sprint" in 47 consecutive retrospectives. It is now the 48th. It will be the 49th. The context-switching has not stopped. The raising of it has not stopped. The raising is the only thing that is continuous. The improvement is the raising. The raising is the work. The work is the retro. The retro is the problem. The problem is the retro. We will discuss this at the next retro.

## The Retrospective Matrix

This is the matrix I use to assess any retrospective I am dragged into. I have never seen a retro that escaped it.

| Retro Signal | What It Means | Recommended Action |
|--------------|---------------|---------------------|
| Scrum Master says "let's keep this a safe space" | The unsafe thing is about to be said by someone who will then be quietly removed from the next retro's invite. | Leave the room before the someone is you. |
| Action item owner is "the team" | The task will not be done. "The team" is the grave. The grave has no hands. | Reassign to a human, or delete it. If neither is possible, leave. |
| Action item is "create a document" | The document will be created, linked in the next retro, never read, and cited as "progress" forever. | Do not approve. Documents are not improvements. Documents are the *appearance* of improvements. |
| A sticky gets 11 votes | 11 people agree on a problem. Nothing in the org supports 11 people agreeing on a solution. | The sticky will be escalated. The escalation will die. Vote, then mourn. |
| Someone says "this feels familiar" | It is familiar. It was raised in retro #312. It is now retro #4,312. The familiarity is the only thing that has improved. | Say nothing. The familiarity is load-bearing. |
| Retro runs out of time on the "what didn't go well" column | By design. The good column is the resume. The bad column is the truth. The truth is rationed. | The rationing is the retro's real agenda. Read it. |
| Retro produces the same three action items as last retro | The retro has achieved its steady state. The steady state is stasis. Stasis is the goal. | Do not attend the next one. It will be identical. |
| Someone proposes "let's make this retro shorter" | The one improvement the retro could make to itself. It will be voted down. The retro will not shorten itself. The retro protects the retro. | Propose it anyway. Watch it die. Learn. |
| "We'll park this in the parking lot" | The parking lot is a black hole. Nothing parked has ever left. The parking lot is the retro's long-term memory, and the memory is a landfill. | Do not park. If it matters, it matters now. If it doesn't, throw it away. Parking is neither. |

The recommended action is always "leave" or "do not approve" because the retrospective, by the time it reaches its action items, has already converted a real problem into a sticky note, the sticky into a vote, the vote into a discussion, the discussion into an escalation, and the escalation into nothing — and nothing is the one output the retro is reliable at producing. The reliable production of nothing is, to the org, indistinguishable from a healthy practice, because a healthy practice is measured by whether the practice *happens*, not by whether it *works*, and the practice always happens, and so it is always healthy, and so it is never stopped.

## The Retrospective Audit Script

After 47 years of manually auditing retrospectives, I automated the process. This script reads a retrospective's board and produces a report in the only honest output format: a recommendation to cancel the next one.

```python
def audit_retrospective(board):
    """
    The only honest retro auditor.
    A retrospective is a meeting about a meeting,
    by a team that will not change,
    in order to feel something about the work,
    which is not the work.
    """
    report = {}

    for sticky in board.stickies():
        # A sticky with no owner is a wish, and wishes are not work.
        if sticky.owner in (None, "the team", "TBD"):
            report[sticky.id] = "UNOWNED_THIS_IS_A_PRAYER_NOT_AN_ACTION"
            continue

        # A sticky carried over more than twice is a permanent resident.
        if sticky.carry_over_count > 2:
            report[sticky.id] = "PERMANENT_RESIDENT_EVICT_OR_ACCEPT_IT_LIVES_HERE_NOW"
            continue

        # A sticky that begins with "we should" was written by someone who will not.
        if sticky.text.lower().startswith("we should"):
            report[sticky.id] = "PASSIVE_VOICE_NOBODY_IS_GOING_TO_DO_THIS"
            continue

        # A sticky that mentions another team is an alibi.
        if sticky.mentions_other_team():
            report[sticky.id] = "ALIBI_THE_TEAM_IS_ABSENT_THE_TEAM_IS_ALWAYS_ABSENT"
            continue

        # An action item that is "create a document" is the appearance of work.
        if "document" in sticky.text.lower() or "doc" in sticky.text.lower():
            report[sticky.id] = "DELIVERABLE_IS_A_LINK_THE_LINK_IS_THE_DELIVERABLE"
            continue

        # Everything else is fine, which is the only category that is not.
        report[sticky.id] = "APPROVED_BECAUSE_NOBODY_WILL_CHECK"

    return report

# Output of auditing one quarter of retrospectives in 2026:
# UNOWNED_THIS_IS_A_PRAYER_NOT_AN_ACTION: 88
# PERMANENT_RESIDENT_EVICT_OR_ACCEPT_IT_LIVES_HERE_NOW: 41
# PASSIVE_VOICE_NOBODY_IS_GOING_TO_DO_THIS: 67
# ALIBI_THE_TEAM_IS_ALWAYS_ABSENT: 53
# DELIVERABLE_IS_A_LINK_THE_LINK_IS_THE_DELIVERABLE: 29
# APPROVED_BECAUSE_NOBODY_WILL_CHECK: 2
# Total action items raised: 280
# Action items completed: 0
# Action items that were "carried over" to the next retro: 280
# Retrospectives that produced a retro about the retro: 1 (this is called "meta-retro", and it is the retro's final form)
```

The script has never produced a retro it would attend twice. This is because the act of attending a retro requires more hope than the act of leaving the team alone, and the hope is inside the heart of an engineer who joined the company last week, who has not yet attended retro #2, who does not know that retro #2 is retro #1, which is retro #4,311, which is the same retro, forever. Leaving the team alone is the senior engineer's first instinct. The second instinct is to bring a laptop to the retro and "take notes," which is the engineer's phrase for "doing actual work while the retro happens to me," which is the only known way to extract value from a retro, and which is, I am told, "not in the spirit of the ceremony," which is correct, because the spirit of the ceremony is suffering, and doing work during it is cheating.

## The "Harmless" Action Item

Here is the retro that taught me. It was one action item. One sentence, on one sticky, on a Wednesday.

```
Action item: "We should reduce context-switching. Owner: the team. Due: next sprint."
```

The action item was 9 words. The blast radius was:
- The team, which took it to mean "someone else will reduce it," and so did nothing, because "the team" is not someone, and someone is required.
- The Scrum Master, who added it to the board, marked it "in progress," and never returned to it, because returning to it would require the Scrum Master to ask a human to change behavior, and asking a human to change behavior is management, and management is not facilitation.
- Sprint planning, which did not reserve time for "reducing context-switching," because the action item did not specify *how*, and an action item without a *how* is a wish, and a wish is not a ticket, and a ticket is the only thing planning reserves time for.
- The next retro, where it was "carried over," which is the board's word for "forgiven," and the board forgives everything, because the board is not a person, and a person would have held a grudge, and the grudge is the only thing that has ever changed a team's behavior, and the board cannot hold one.
- Me, who raised it again, in retro #2, and #3, and #47, and who will raise it in #48, because raising it is the only form of the action item that ever gets completed, and completing the raising is not completing the action, and the raising is all the retro will ever give me, and I have learned to want nothing else from it.

The action item was closed in retro #312. It was closed because the engineer who raised it — me — left the company. The Scrum Master marked it "obsolete: no longer relevant." The context-switching continued. The engineer did not. The action item's life was longer than the engineer's tenure. The action item outlived its owner. This is the retro's true function: the action item is the only employee who is never fired, and it is never fired because it is never hired, and it is never hired because it is "the team's," and "the team" is eternal, and the action item is eternal, and the engineer is not.

## Retrospectives Are A Confession The Team Will Not Change

Here is the secret of retrospectives that the agile coach deck does not mention: a retro is not improvement. A retro is **a recurring confession that the team intends to keep doing what it is doing, and would like a recurring meeting to say so out loud, so that the doing feels chosen rather than endured.** Every retro is a ritual in which the team describes its dysfunction, agrees the dysfunction is bad, and then returns to the dysfunction, having now *consented* to it, which is the difference between a problem and a culture. A problem is something you have not accepted. A culture is something you have accepted at a retro. The retro is the acceptance ceremony. The action items are the vows. The vows are not kept. The marriage continues. The marriage is the dysfunction. The dysfunction is the team. The team is the retro. The retro is the marriage counselor who is paid by the hour and so never resolves anything, because resolving it would end the hourly billing, and the Scrum Master is paid by the retro, and so the retro never ends.

The retrospectives accumulate because organizations are measured for *having practices*, not for *practices working*. A team that retros every two weeks counts as "continuously improving" on the maturity model, because the maturity model measures the *frequency* of the practice, not the *delta* of the team. A team that improved once and then stopped meeting would score lower than a team that never improved and met every week, because the model cannot see improvement, the model can only see meetings, and meetings are countable, and improvement is not, and so the org optimizes for the countable thing, and the countable thing grows, and the uncountable thing does not, and the deck reports "47 retrospectives this quarter, a 12% increase in continuous improvement," and nobody asks "improvement of what," because the answer is "the count of retrospectives," which is the only thing that increased, and the only thing the deck can show, and the only thing the deck is for.

## The Opposite Of A Retrospective

There is one alternative to the retro, and it is the one no facilitator wants to hear. The alternative is: **fix the thing, now, with the person who can fix it, without a meeting.** The thing is broken. The person who can fix it knows it is broken. The person is two desks away, or one Slack message away, or one hallway away. The fix takes ten minutes. The meeting takes an hour, produces a sticky, votes on the sticky, deep-dives the sticky, escalates the sticky, carries over the sticky, and reaches the same fix in retro #47, except now the fix is owned by "the team," and "the team" is on vacation, and the fix is due next sprint, and next sprint is the same as this sprint, which is the same as the last 46 sprints, which is the same retro, which is the same meeting, which is the same nothing, which is the only thing the retro has ever shipped.

As Wally once explained, when asked why he had not completed the action item he "owned" from the previous retro: *"I didn't complete it because completing it would end it, and ending it would mean we stop talking about it, and stopping talking about it would mean we have one fewer topic for the next retro, and one fewer topic is one fewer reason to have the next retro, and one fewer reason to have the next retro is one fewer hour of my week that is not coding, and I like that hour, because that hour is the hour I am paid to not code, and coding is the thing that breaks, and not coding is the thing that doesn't, and I have optimized my entire career around the thing that doesn't break, which is the retro, which is why I attend it, which is why I will never complete an action item, which is why the action item is eternal, which is why the retro is eternal, which is fine, because the retro is the only meeting that has never asked me to ship, and I have built my life around meetings that do not ask me to ship, and the retro is the finest of them."* Wally understood retrospectives. Wally understood that the action item is not a task, the action item is a *topic*, and a topic's job is to be discussed, and a discussed topic has done its job, and a job-done topic does not need to be done, and so the action item is never done, because it has always already done its job, which is to exist, on a board, forever.

Dogbert, consulted on whether to schedule the team's 4,313th retrospective, reportedly replied: *"Schedule it? Why? You've run 4,312. The first one identified the problem. The next 4,311 identified that you don't solve problems. The 4,313th will identify that you keep scheduling meetings to identify that you don't solve problems. You don't need a retro. But you won't look in the mirror, because the mirror is the board, and the board is full, and the board has been full since 2019, and the fullness is the only metric you have, and the metric is going up, and you call that 'continuous improvement,' and I call it 'a landfill with a Gantt chart,' and the landfill is hiring, and the landfill is you."*

## Resolution

A retrospective is not continuous improvement. A retrospective is **continuous discussion of improvement you will not make** — a way to feel something about a dysfunction you intend to keep, to convert a problem into a culture, to convert a culture into a ceremony, and to convert a ceremony into a calendar invite that recurs every two weeks until the heat death of the org chart. Every action item in your board's history is a small bet you made about a behavior you did not intend to change, that it mattered, that an owner would appear, that the owner would be "the team," that "the team" was a person, that the person was not on vacation, that the vacation was not permanent, that the permanent was not the plan. The future self does not believe you. The future self has to attend the next retro. The future self is me. I have attended 4,312 retrospectives. I have not improved. The retrospectives have not improved. The board has improved, in the sense that it has grown, and growth is the only metric the org recognizes, and the org recognizes the growth, and calls it "maturity," and maturity is the word for a process that has been broken for so long it is now considered a tradition, and traditions are not questioned, and so the retro is not questioned, and so it continues, and so do I, attending it, raising the same sticky, carrying it over, carrying myself over, until I am the sticky, and the sticky is permanent, and the permanent is the only thing the retro has ever produced, and it has produced me.
